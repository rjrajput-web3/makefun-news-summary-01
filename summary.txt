World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Ukraine, Russia and the United States held a second day of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi; President Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive and quite substantive", and officials said the two sides agreed a large prisoner-of-war exchange as the talks closed (BBC). NATO scrambled jets overnight after at least four drones strayed into Baltic states' airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia.

World leaders began speaking at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where UN Secretary-General Guterres warned the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction" (AFP).

In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire on Tuesday after striking naval mines; monitors counted at least 37 attacks on shipping there in November, the most since the nine-month war began. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris the economic campaign against Iran was devastating enough that no major combat operations were needed now; Tehran said it was still awaiting a US answer to its seven conditions for talks.

The Democratic Republic of Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and at least 5,387 deaths, the second-largest outbreak on record (government data, Tuesday; WHO).

In Washington, the House voted 211-204 on Tuesday to refer an impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it, and House Republicans chose Representative Jim Jordan as their new leader. Nvidia fell 5.4 percent on Tuesday to $169.04, after Chinese procurement records showed a defense-linked university bought two of its H200 chips.

Also: a UK court struck down Trinidad and Tobago's centuries-old ban on gay sex; former Malaysian PM Najib Razak began house arrest after four years in prison over the 1MDB scandal; and Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza on Tuesday, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said.
